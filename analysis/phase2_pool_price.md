# Phase 2: pool and price evidence (free sources only)

Date: 2026-11-06. Repo values are from vstress/data.py. Evidence grade: A = primary government document read in full text; B = vendor's own page or a secondary source quoting a primary; C = web-search snippet or third-party blog, not independently verified (re-check before relying on it).

Five ventures were picked from the 14 by $/delivery-hour in analysis/reverse_report.txt, then filtered for whether a public price anchor exists. Customs HTS (4.33x bar), Trademark watch (5.20x), Freight audit (3.96x), Medical billing denials (3.46x), Grant compliance (3.90x). Insurance subrogation (4.04x) is a runner-up, noted at the end.

## 1. Customs pool question: firms or individuals?

Answer: the buyer pool is about 2,365 firms, not 16,170 individuals and not 5,200. Nothing found supports 5,200.

| Quantity | Value | Definition | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active licensed brokers, Jan 2022 | 15,226 | individuals plus a few corporate licenses | CBP final rule, Federal Register 2022-22445, "As of January 2022, there are 15,226 active, licensed customs brokers": https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-10-18/html/2022-22445.htm | A |
| Active licensed brokers, current | ~14,454 | same | https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/becoming-customs-broker | A |
| Brokers holding at least one permit | 2,365 (15.5%) | each permit holder is a filing entity | same rule: "2,365 permitted brokers... the majority of brokers never apply for their own permit and work under the auspices of a corporate permit" | A |
| Corporate (firm) permit holders | ~2% of all brokers, about 305 | large multi-broker firms | same rule: "Approximately two percent of brokers hold a corporate permit, meaning 13.5 percent hold individual permits" | A |
| Individual permit holders | ~13.5%, about 2,060 | solo or small shops filing in their own name | same | A |
| Broker directory | ~2,500 firms | commercial directory | repo's own report (not re-verified here) | C |
| Clutch directory | ~762 US companies | marketing directory, incomplete | https://clutch.co/us/logistics/customs-brokers | C |

Reasoning. A product priced per firm sells to whoever files entries and holds the account, which is the permit holder. CBP's own count of permitted entities (2,365) agrees with the ~2,500 directory figure. The 16,170 and 15,226 counts are people, and most of them are employees of a corporate permit holder who will not buy separately. The 5,200 figure matches no source I found.

Consequence for the repo math. At $750/mo, 2,365 firms and 1.1% conversion, market = $19.5k/mo, which is below the $20k bar. The price that clears the bar at 1.1% is $769/mo. At the default 0.6% conversion it is $1,408/mo. The only evidenced venture is therefore marginal on the corrected pool.

## 3. What the numbers change

1. Customs is still the best-evidenced venture, but the pool is 2,365 firms, and $750 at 1.1% gives $19.5k/mo, 2.4% short of the bar. It clears only if the real price is about $770 or higher.
2. Trademark watch has the largest apparent arithmetic edge (5.20x) and the worst price evidence: the repo's $300/mo is above the whole market of $3-$33/mo. Drop it or reprice to a different service.
3. Grant compliance has the best pool and a plausible price band ($350-$500/mo), but at $350-$500 and 4 hours per account the rate is $88-$125 per hour, still above the $57.74 bar.
4. Freight audit and medical denials are priced by outcome in the market (contingency 20-50% of recovered, or per invoice). A flat monthly price for either has no anchor.
5. At the recommended anchors, $/hour is: Customs $750/3.0 = $250 (unchanged); Grant $425/4.0 = $106; Trademark $33/1.0 = $33; denials at $300/provider for a 3-provider practice with 5 h = $180 (assumes 3 providers, unsourced); Freight $350/3.5 = $100 plus contingency upside.

## 4. Runner-up: insurance subrogation

- Pool: 2,578 US property and casualty carriers (Vertical IQ, C, methodology unverified) https://verticaliq.com/?p=273813 . Definition: carrier companies, not groups.
- Price points: contingency 15-18% is the common insurer-vendor rate (Claims Journal) https://www.claimsjournal.com/columns/road-to-recovery/2022/05/23/310582.htm ; a public-agency contract pays 30%, rising to 45% above a recovery threshold https://lfportal.everettwa.gov/WebLink/0/doc/2096094/Page29.aspx . Both are contingency, so the repo's $3,500/mo flat fee has no incumbent anchor.
- Anchor: 15-20% of recovery, with a possible flat platform fee.

## 5. Gaps and limits

- Census CBP API requires a key, so firm counts from NAICS 488510, 6211, 524291 were not pulled. Obtain a free key and replace the grade C pool numbers.
- Zonos pricing was seen only in a search snippet; the fetched docs page returned no text.
- No free source gives a count of trademark owners, or of shippers with recurring LTL spend. Both pools in the repo are unsupported.
- The customs rule counts are as of January 2022; the CBP page gives ~14,454 now, so the firm count is probably slightly lower today.
